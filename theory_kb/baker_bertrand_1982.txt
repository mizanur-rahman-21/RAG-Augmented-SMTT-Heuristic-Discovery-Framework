Title: A Dynamic Priority Rule for Scheduling Against Due-Dates
Authors: K. R. Baker and J. W. M. Bertrand (1982)
Source: Journal of Operations Management, 3(1), 37-42.

Key Insights for Heuristic Design:
1. The Modified Due Date (MDD) rule defines a dynamic priority:
   priority_j(t) = max(t + p_j, d_j)
2. At scheduling time t, if job j would complete after its due date (t + p_j >= d_j), its effective priority is t + p_j. Minimizing t + p_j across unscheduled jobs is equivalent to scheduling the shortest processing time job (SPT).
3. If job j would complete before its due date (t + p_j < d_j), its effective priority is d_j, which corresponds to scheduling the earliest due date job (EDD).
4. MDD acts as an adaptive hybrid that automatically transitions from EDD (slack preservation) to SPT (tardiness minimization) as the machine clock advances.
5. Simple convex combinations of the form w1*(t + p_j) + w2*d_j embedded inside max() often collapse mathematically to standard MDD whenever w1 + w2 = 1.
