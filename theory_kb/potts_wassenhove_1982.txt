Title: A Decomposition Algorithm for the Single Machine Total Tardiness Problem
Authors: C. N. Potts and L. N. Van Wassenhove (1982)
Source: Operations Research, 30(2), 378-389.

Key Insights for Heuristic Design:
1. In single-machine total tardiness scheduling (1 || sum Tj), the trade-off between processing time p_j and due date d_j is fundamentally non-linear.
2. If all jobs are tardy (t >= max(d_j)), the total tardiness objective is equivalent to minimizing sum of completion times, for which Shortest Processing Time (SPT) is strictly optimal.
3. If no jobs are tardy (C_j <= d_j for all j), Earliest Due Date (EDD) is strictly optimal.
4. For instances with intermediate due date tightness (measured by Tardiness Factor TF and Relative Due Date Range RDD), optimal job orderings balance SPT-like and EDD-like behavior.
5. Exact solution through dynamic programming follows the backward subset recursion:
   V(J) = min_{j in J} [ V(J \ {j}) + max(0, C(J) - d_j) ], where C(J) = sum_{k in J} p_k.
